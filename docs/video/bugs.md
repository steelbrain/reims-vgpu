# Bugs and deferred work

Everything known to be wrong, untested or put off in the video devices' host side, so it can be picked up later without being rediscovered.

**Each entry says:**
- what is wrong;
- the evidence: a run, a test or a source line, tagged **measured** or **read** as [testing.md](../testing.md), "Tags", says;
- why it waits;
- what would fix it;
- who has it, where anyone does.

**Keeping it current:**
- An entry is added in the commit that defers the work.
- It is deleted in the commit that fixes it. An ID is not used again, since other docs and the code cite entries by it.

## Open bugs

### B14. A host session has no VRAM budget

- **What:** 16 decoder sessions, the bound of F7 (`MAX_SESSIONS`, `crates/reims-vgpu-video-core/src/host/guest_bounds.rs`), can each hold a DPB of driver-maximum surfaces: about 480 MB for 4096x4096 H.264 with 16 frames (F7), so 7.7 GB from one guest on a shared GPU.
- **Evidence:** **read**: the VA-API H.264 decoder's `configure` (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/h264.rs`) makes `dpb_frames + 1 + SPARE_SURFACES` surfaces at the SPS's size. **Measured** on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU (16,303 MiB, driver 615.71.09), 2026-10-24: one decoder of a 7680x4320 HEVC Main 10 stream held 1,020 MiB of VRAM, with the DPB its SPS asks for; one process with one held 1,216 MiB, with two 2,236 MiB. NVDEC's surfaces are the DPB plus 3 at the stream's size, so 16 sessions of such a stream would ask for the whole GPU, the host desktop's memory included. The probe stopped at two.
- **Why it waits:** no budget, as Apple's host has none. The host puts no limit of its own on how much of its hardware one VM holds (B31, B39).
- **Fix, if that changes:** a budget of surface bytes per host session, or fewer sessions.

### B26. Some guest command buffers run for seconds on a discrete GPU, over guest memory

- **What:** on an RTX 5080 Laptop GPU a few of an x86_64 guest's command buffers run on the GPU for seconds, and the command buffers committed behind them wait. In every boot measured, a VideoToolbox pixel transfer kernel, one dispatch, runs 2.4 to 2.7 s, about 10 s after the guest's SSH is up. In the boots where mediaanalysisd runs its machine-learning analysis, 44 to 52 s later, one of its command buffers, of 452 dispatches, runs 2.4 to 3.0 s, and one exec of 794 dispatches 23.1 to 29.2 s. The guest's GPU watchdog acts on that exec: about 10 s into it the guest restarts its GPU, and resumes FIFO 1 until it completes. Waiters on stamps behind these command buffers log "waiter N waited 2s for stamp N to reach ..." from `reims_vgpu_core::device::waits`, with their stamp wait reports. 31 boots on a Radeon 780M logged no such wait. At metal2vulkan 27935d575e the same command buffers take 9 to 10 ms, 309 to 359 ms and 565 to 591 ms, and no boot restarts its GPU (below).
- **"resumed FIFO 1 was not faulted"** is the guest resuming a child FIFO whose fault bit is clear (`resume_child_fifo`, `crates/reims-vgpu-core/src/device/fifos.rs`, **read**): the device logs the warning, reports its stamp waits within the process's budget of nine, and resumes the child FIFO. Here the guest's watchdog resumes FIFO 1, which is not faulted but busy with the long exec: a symptom, not a fault.
- **Evidence** (**measured**, driver 615.71.09, 2026-10-25): seven boots of that guest to its desktop as the e2e checks boot it (`rvgv-msi-1`), five from 62e19c8ef and two from `steelbrain/video-pin` (metal2vulkan 2e4c21ff6f), each with a probe that logged every exec in flight for over 1.5 s: its task, stamp, waits, dispatches and resources.   - **What runs for seconds: stores into host memory that leave gaps, on the RTX 5080 Laptop GPU** (**measured**, 2026-10-26, metal2vulkan 67c64f99d8; the RTX 5080 Laptop GPU on driver 615.71.09, its link at its slot's PCIe 5.0 x8 under load; the RX 7900 XTX and the Radeon 780M on RADV, Mesa 26.2.4):
    - The transfer kernel's three textures are IOSurfaces. The Vulkan platform makes a texture on a surface over a buffer over the surface's pages (`new_texture_on_surface`, `crates/reims-vgpu-vulkan/src/gpu/device.rs`), and the runtime makes no image that aliases a caller's host pages, as such an image starts `UNDEFINED` and Vulkan lets its first move read and write every byte it is bound to. It holds the texture as a shadowed texture instead: an image of its own, whose texels it copies in from the bytes before each use and, after a command writes through it, merges back into them, storing only the words that changed (**read**, metal2vulkan 67c64f99d8, `texture.rs`, `shadow_merge.rs`; announced as `metal2vulkan-degrade-shadowed-texture`).

### B28. The host sends no per-frame histogram (`,dp2`)

- **What:** Apple's AVD sends a 544-byte `,dp2` with every decoded frame, which the guest sets as its surface's data property 2 with `IOSurfaceSetDataProperty` (**read** from Apple's guest code); ours sends none ([devices.md](devices.md), "Colour and HDR" and "Where we differ from Apple").
- **Evidence:** **measured** with a probe of Apple's host session on the M3 Max (macOS 27.0.1), 29 streams of every codec and depth, 2026-10-24: the bytes `1tshdvaa`, six 32-bit words (one that differs between sessions, a frame counter from 1, 128, 32, then 0), then 128 little-endian 32-bit counts that sum to the frame's pixels (147,456 at 512x288) and are the same for two streams of the same pictures with other colour tags.
- **Why it waits:** left out on purpose for x86_64 guests. What the 128 bins count (luma alone or more, how 8 and 10 bits and the range map to them) is not measured, and no reader of data property 2 is known in the guest. Its magic, `1tshdvaa`, is in none of the seven `dyld_shared_cache_x86_64h` files of a 25G241 guest, in either byte order, nor is `hst1` (**measured**, `grep -c -a -F`), so no x86_64 guest code knows the format by its tag. It looks to be AVD's statistic for Apple silicon's display, which an x86_64 guest lacks. A frame without it decodes, and its colour attachments are the same (Apple's carries none).
- **Fix:** look for a reader in an arm64 guest's frameworks: `IOSurfaceGetDataProperty` callers with index 2. If one matters, measure the bins on Apple's host with flat streams of known samples (one luma value a stream, at 8 and 10 bits, video and full range), then count them in core's copy into the guest's surface, which reads every luma sample already, and send `,dp2` from core for every backend.

### B90. Where the JPEG engine cannot write host memory, its maps hold the display's lock for milliseconds

- **What:** the JPEG engine reads its outputs from host memory of ours, written there whole, or, past udmabuf's `size_limit_mb` (64 MiB at its stock value), a strip of rows at a time, a 4:2:2 copy's through an alias of its surface ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "Surfaces and readback"). Where it cannot, it reads them through the driver's derived image (`vaDeriveImage`, `vaMapBuffer`), or `vaGetImage`: where no host memory can be made at all, as in a process that may not open `/dev/udmabuf`; on a driver other than radeonsi; past udmabuf's limit on a driver with no video processing; and from the first decode on which a driver call writing host memory failed. Mesa's VA frontend makes the map under the display's lock, and radeonsi copies the surface on the GPU for it and waits for the copy (U22). While it maps, every other session's calls on the display wait for it, through the display's gate ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "The display's calls").
- **Evidence:** **measured** on the RX 7900 XTX and the Radeon 780M (Mesa 26.2.4, 2026-10-26), a thread decoding a JPEG over and over beside a 4K H.264 decode on one display (720 `decode` calls a run, each frame waited for), the call thread's holds of the gate and the H.264 thread's waits for it counted by instrumenting `src/va/calls.rs` (not in the tree), in the trees that read these outputs through derived maps:
  - 8192x8192 4:2:0 copied out: 85 to 91 maps a run on the RX 7900 XTX, 19.7 to 21.6 ms at the mean and 44 to 45 ms at most, and 77 to 78 on the Radeon 780M, 5.5 to 6.7 ms and 34 to 59 ms, the H.264 thread waiting for the gate 84 to 90 and 76 to 78 times a run.
  - 4:2:2 copied into `422f`, the H.264 decoder kept across the passes: beside 8192x8192, 43 maps a run on the RX 7900 XTX, 20.5 to 20.6 ms at the mean, and 40 to 41 on the Radeon 780M, 5.1 to 5.2 ms; beside 12288x8192, 30 of 30.2 ms, and 28 to 30 of 7.3 to 7.4 ms. The H.264 thread waited for the gate once a map, and the map's hold set the RX 7900 XTX's longest `decode` calls, 27.9 to 29.0 ms and 38.6 to 39.7 ms against 9.8 to 10.3 alone.
  - Read from host memory, whole or in strips, none of these outputs makes a call that holds the gate ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "What the gate costs").
  - `vaGetImage` takes a rectangle, and a program on libva alone read a YUY2 surface with it a band of 32 MiB at a time, every byte right: each call held the lock 13.1 to 14.8 ms at the mean, and 16.8 to 20.3 at most, for a band of 8192x8192 on the RX 7900 XTX, and 9.5 to 10.2 and 13.5 to 15.5 ms on the Radeon 780M, four a decode, each two thirds of a whole map's 20.5 ms on the RX 7900 XTX and twice its 5.2 ms on the Radeon 780M, since it copies the band into the image on the CPU with the lock held (U22).
- **Why it waits:** on radeonsi at udmabuf's stock limits, every output of the engine's is read from host memory; what is left is a host where udmabuf cannot be opened, the other drivers, whose maps are not measured, and a fallback no run has taken.
- **Fix:** `vaGetImage` of bands small enough that each hold is short, more calls a decode (not tried); or Mesa's, U22.

### B92. After memory of the process's GPU is freed, its next VA-API work waits for every job then on the GPU

- **What:** a surface or buffer destroyed unmaps its memory from the process's GPU VM, and amdgpu has every later submission of the VM, on any ring, wait for every job the VM had in flight at the unmap (U23). RADV's jobs in the process are of that VM, a guest's command buffers on the display among them, so a VA-API frame submitted after an unmap waits for the work the GPU had then, and fails by its deadline as stalled where that work runs past it. The JPEG engine keeps the surfaces of its decodes for later ones of the same shape, so that a run of decodes frees nothing ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "Surfaces and readback"); it still destroys the least recently used past its 64 MiB, and a failed decode's, and every other unmap of the process, another session's or metal2vulkan's runtime's, still has the next VA-API work wait.
  - **A new decoder's first picture waits on the CPU.** radeonsi submits the job that begins a decoder's session in the decoder's first `vaEndPicture`, and waits there for it, with the display's lock held (U24). After an unmap that job waits for every job then on the GPU, and the call waits as long: a decoder made just after another session let go of its own, beside a JPEG decode, a guest's command buffers or a playing session's work. The backend makes a decoder's first `vaEndPicture` on the display's call thread, by the frame's deadline (`render_decode`, `crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/buffer.rs`), so past it the frame fails as stalled, and the display's other calls wait behind it for the gate, each by its own deadline.

## Upstream bugs

Bugs in drivers, libraries and tools that our code or our checks work around.

- **U1. ffmpeg's native HEVC decoder** is wrong on `--ctu 16` streams with SAO and deblocking. Our HEVC check uses x265's reconstruction as a second reference because of it ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "Testing HEVC").
- **U2. libva's header.** `VAIQMatrixBufferHEVC`'s documentation gives two orders for the scaling lists; radeonsi needs raster, which ours sends (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/hevc.rs`).
- **U3. radeonsi rewrites the packed parameter sets an encoder gives it.** With packed SPS, PPS (and VPS) and slice headers, the coded buffer holds parameter sets of the driver's own: H.264 High's `transform_8x8_mode_flag` goes from 1 to 0 and its VUI restriction fields change; HEVC's height goes from 1080 to 1088 with a conformance window, its transform depths from 0 to 4 against the 0..0 the driver reports, and its bitstream restriction is dropped. The VPS and HEVC PPS come out as packed. With no packed slice header the coded buffer holds no parameter set at all. So our configuration record is read from what the hardware emits ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "The encoders"). **Measured** with a libva program of our own, which encodes one frame and compares, on an RX 7900 XTX and a Radeon 780M alike (Mesa 26.2.4, libva 2.24.1, 2026-10-24).
- **U4. libvpx's VP9 encoder.** In realtime mode, an ROI map with a skipped segment held to INTRA or GOLDEN writes inter frames libvpx's own decoder refuses, so no test stream covers that pair (T10).
- **U5. libva-nvidia-driver 0.0.18** answers `VAConfigAttribMaxPictureWidth` and `Height` with 0 on any thread but the one that opened the display (**measured** with a libva program of our own, 2026-10-24: 8192x8192 on the opening thread, 0x0 on a second one, and 8192x8192 again on the first; radeonsi the same on both). Our backend asks every query on the opening thread and keeps the answers (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/caps.rs`), so it does not depend on it.
- **U6. NVENC with lookahead.** In synchronous mode with lookahead on (P6 and P7 at high quality), after `NV_ENC_SUCCESS` only the oldest pending output locks, though the header says all of them do, and frames are lost. The NVIDIA backend turns lookahead off (D12).
- **U7. NVDEC with AV1 and VP9 frames of a 4n+3 side.** Through NVIDIA's own parser, both codecs decode such frames wrongly at 8 and 10 bits. Our VP9 decoder decodes them right, with its surfaces and maps rounded up to even sides and the frame's own size in the picture parameters, so the parser's VP9 parameters may be the cause there; our AV1 decoder decodes them as wrongly as the parser does, so the NVIDIA backend refuses them, with -12911 (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-nvidia/src/decode.rs`).
- **U8. ffmpeg's H.264 decoder leaves frames out after a frame number gap that wraps.** On a stream whose SPS allows gaps, with order count type 2 and an I picture just after a gap of 5, ffmpeg n9.0.2 output 50 of 60 frames, missing 30 to 39; with a gap of 9 and four references, 54, missing 30 to 35 (**measured**, 2026-10-24). Apple's AVD, our VA-API decoder and the source's own decode agree on all 60. Streams written from scratch found the condition: the skipped frame numbers wrap past MaxFrameNum (16 here, from frame_num 13), and 15 - G frames are lost for a gap of G, while the same gaps from frame_num 0 lose none. The cause, read in ffmpeg's `h264_slice.c`, is that the gap code moves `prev_frame_num` past the wrap without adding MaxFrameNum to `prev_frame_num_offset`, so the pictures after the gap get order counts one MaxFrameNum low and are dropped as out of order; with it added, all 52 of the streams' decodes gave 60 frames. The gap streams' reference is therefore their source's (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/tools/h264-check.sh`).
- **U9. libvpx's SVC encoder writes streams its own decoder refuses.** With three spatial layers, three temporal layers (the 0212 pattern) and `VP9E_SET_SVC_INTER_LAYER_PRED` off, at realtime CBR, libvpx 1.17.0's encoder wrote an inter frame whose every reference is out of VP9's size range, and vpxdec stopped there: "Referenced frame has invalid size" (`vp9-svc 3 3 1 0` and `3 3 1 1`, 2026-10-24; B40). A report needs a program written from scratch on libvpx's API, and a check of libvpx's current release.
- **U10. radeonsi's video processing writes a bottom chroma siting as a top one.** A BGRA to NV12 run given `VA_CHROMA_SITING_VERTICAL_BOTTOM` gives the Cb plane, byte for byte, that `VERTICAL_TOP` gives, with either horizontal siting; top, centre and both horizontal sitings land where they say (**measured**, Mesa 26.2.4, libva 2.24.1, an RX 7900 XTX and a Radeon 780M, 2026-10-24). Nothing of ours names a siting to radeonsi now: the scaler device transforms on its own kernel ([scaler.md](scaler.md), "The kernel"). libva's `BOTTOM` is 0x03, `TOP`'s and `CENTER`'s bits together; that Mesa takes it as `TOP` for that reason is a guess, not read in Mesa's source.
- **U11. NVDEC decodes VP9 spatial layers wrongly.** libvpx's three spatial layers with inter-layer prediction (`vp9-svc 3 1 0 0`, 1280x720) went through the NVIDIA backend with surfaces of the top layer's size. The first five frames equalled libvpx's; then the second top-layer frame was wrong in its two top-left 64x64 blocks, and the error spread to 22.4 dB by the 150th top-layer frame, with no status (**measured**, RTX 5080 Laptop GPU, driver 615.71.09, 2026-10-24; B40, D16). VCN decodes the same streams right through the same codec crate. NVIDIA's own parser calls every frame after the third 1280x720, so a report needs a program written from scratch that drives NVDEC with VP9 headers of its own parsing.
- **U12. libaom shows forward key frames it codes not showable.** With `--enable-fwd-kf=1 --fwd-kf-dist=16` and key frame filtering at its default, libaom 3.15.1 and 3.15.2 code a forward key frame `showable_frame` 0 and show it later with `show_existing_frame`, which 6.8.2 does not allow: aomdec stops at that frame ("Buffer does not contain a showable frame") and the dav1d command line at 48 of 60, while ffmpeg's libdav1d and Apple's host decode all 60 ([devices.md](devices.md), "AV1"). With `--enable-keyframe-filtering=0` the stream is valid. Ours shows such a frame, as Apple's host does; `crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/tools/av1-check.sh`'s aom-360p-fwdkf-filtered is such a stream, checked against libdav1d alone, and aom-720p-fwdkf the valid one.
- **U13. kvazaar writes no first access unit when it codes fields with a GOP of 8 or 16.** With `--source-scan-type tff` or `bff` and `--gop 8` (`--preset fast`'s) or `--gop 16` (the default), kvazaar 2.3.2 (CachyOS's 2.3.2-2.1) wrote 59 field pictures for 30 frames and no VPS, SPS, PPS or IDR picture: the stream starts with a TRAIL_R picture, and ffmpeg and our parser cannot open it ("PPS 0 has not been received"). With `--gop 0` or a low-delay GOP it wrote all 60, starting with the parameter sets and an IDR picture, and those decode. `--debug` with fields aborted on `recon_buffer_size == 0` (`encmain.c:747`) with `--gop 8`, crashed with `--gop 0` on a plain `testsrc2`, and with `lp-g4d3t1` wrote pictures that are not the decoded fields (10.8 dB against the decode, which is 36.8 dB against the source). `hevc-check.sh`'s kvz-720p-fields uses `lp-g4d3t1`, and checks it against ffmpeg's decode with kvazaar's MD5 SEI instead of `--debug`.
- **U14. radeonsi's VBR and CBR pass the HRD buffer they are given.** With an HRD buffer of 0.5 Mbit and VBR or CBR at 1 Mbit/s, the key frames of a picture whose key frames cost most came out 2.09 to 2.44 times the buffer (**measured** with a libva program of our own on an RX 7900 XTX and a Radeon 780M, Mesa 26.2.4, libva 2.24.1, 2026-10-24). Mesa sends the firmware the buffer and `enforce_hrd` 1, and `VAEncMiscParameterTypeMaxFrameSize` at the buffer left the first key frame where it was. Our VA-API streams under `DataRateLimits` do not depend on it: the driver runs a constant QP, and ours picks each picture's ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "Rate control under `DataRateLimits`").
- **U15. radeonsi decodes narrow 10-bit VP9 and AV1 inter frames wrongly.** On an RX 7900 XTX and a Radeon 780M (Navi 31 and Phoenix, Mesa 26.2.4, **measured** 2026-10-24, again on the 780M 2026-10-25), a 10-bit VP9 Profile 2 or AV1 stream 32 or fewer samples wide decodes its key frame right and its inter frames wrongly, with no error: ffmpeg n9.0.2's `-hwaccel vaapi` decode of vpxenc's and aomenc's 10-frame streams of `testsrc2` at 32x32 equalled its software decode in 1 frame of 10, at 48x48 in 10 of 10, and ours through the host session the same. 8-bit streams decode right from the driver's smallest, 16x16, and 10-bit ones from 33 wide (34x64 checked in P010; odd widths at 10 bits were not checked). The VA-API backend refuses such a session at `dsta` with -12911, as Apple's decoder refuses a size it does not decode, so the guest decodes it itself (`NARROWEST_DEEP`, `crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/backend.rs`). - **D22. What zero copy leaves to the CPU's copy** ([core.md](core.md), "Zero copy"). Zero copy keeps what the guest observes as it was: its replies, its holds, the order of writes into its memory, and the layout `Dpba` asks for. Each case below is copied by the CPU, with its reason in the log once a session:
  - **Rows radeonsi does not take: 1080p 8-bit, 360p, 1366x766.** radeonsi imports rows of a multiple of 256 bytes alone (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/zero_copy.rs`), and Apple's `Dpba` asks for 64-byte alignment, so the guest's 1080p 8-bit rows are 1920 bytes (**measured** in an x86_64 guest: `rows of 1920 bytes`). `Dpba` keeps Apple's layout, so a layout of our own is not the fix. What would fix it: a radeonsi that takes such rows (not asked upstream: it refuses them with `VA_STATUS_ERROR_ALLOCATION_FAILED` rather than writing them wrong, and the staged copy back's own linear surfaces keep to 256 bytes too). The copy costs 0.24 ms of a 1080p frame on an RX 7900 XTX (**measured** in an x86_64 guest).
  - **Odd sizes on radeonsi.** Into an odd-sized import, every sample past the first chroma pair came out other than the probe expected, at 8 and 10 bits, with nothing written outside the planes (**measured** with a libva probe of our own at 641x361; [vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "Zero copy"), so frames of an odd width or height are declined. Why is not known: radeonsi may lay an odd-sized linear surface out otherwise than its descriptor says, or the probe may expect the wrong samples at the odd edge. What would tell: the probe's import read back through `vaGetImage` beside its bytes. They stay on the CPU's copy as a residual: they are rare, the copy of such a frame is small, and splitting it, the GPU writing the even part and the CPU the last column and row, would still copy and would rest on a layout radeonsi writes wrongly for a reason not known.
  - **A buffer past 64 strips at the host's udmabuf limits.** A buffer is imported in strips of rows, as many as udmabuf's `list_limit` and `size_limit_mb` call for ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "What udmabuf takes"): at the stock limits, 1,024 runs and 64 MiB, a guest's buffer takes one to four strips up to 4K and 17 or 18 at 8192x4352 P010, so only a host whose limits are below the stock ones meets it. What would fix it: more strips, at a run's fixed cost each. Nothing at the stock limits needs it.
  - **8-bit AV1 on the RX 7900 XTX, one frame at a time.** There the device's write of a 4K frame took 8.73 ms, against the CPU path's read back and copy, 6.68 ms, and the stand-in guest ran 93 fps on against 117 off; HEVC's write took 0.7 ms less than its CPU path on the same GPU, and the Radeon 780M's AV1 0.1 ms less (**measured** at the stock limits, three runs each, 2026-10-25; [vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "What it saves"). Part is the strips: in one strip over runs of eight pages, AV1's write took 1.3 ms less, HEVC's 0.4 ms less. Why AV1's runs cost more on that GPU alone is not known: radeonsi may lay its AV1 decode surfaces out otherwise, so that a run of part of one reads more of it. What would tell: a run's time against the rows it reads, from AV1 and HEVC decode surfaces, on both GPUs. It stays on: the frame is still past 4K60, the CPU's 1.2 ms copy is saved, and zero copy is on wherever a backend allows it.
  - **Whether VA-API's zero copy should stay on where decoding ahead hides the copy.** With the copy's wait outside the driver's lock ([vaapi.md](vaapi.md), "The driver's lock"), the CPU path's copy runs beside the GPU's next decode, and the stand-in's asynchronous 4K sessions ran 0% to 12% faster with zero copy off on both AMD GPUs: the device's write into the guest's pages took the GPU 1.0 to 3.1 ms longer than the staged run into our own memory (**measured**, 2026-10-25). Why is not known: the stand-in's pages lie one a run, as a guest's do, and the GPU's page tables may map them in smaller pieces than our own memfd's, besides the strips' runs. Zero copy saves the CPU 1.2 to 3.1 ms of each such frame, and both ways run past 4K60. It stays on, as zero copy is wherever a backend allows it, until a guest measures it asynchronously on and off at the stock limits: its frame rate and its host's CPU.
  - **VA-API drivers other than radeonsi.** NVIDIA's writes nothing and answers success (U16); the NVIDIA backend is the way there. Intel's drivers are not measured: a driver joins `DRIVERS_WRITING_IMPORTS` (`crates/reims-vgpu-video-vaapi/src/va/display.rs`) once a run that writes into its imports and reads its frames back through `vaGetImage` passes on it.
  - **Real guests' buffers on the VideoToolbox backend: on, unmeasured** ([videotoolbox.md](videotoolbox.md), "Zero copy"). The backend writes a frame into a lent IOSurface by a pixel transfer, and in the stand-in guest wrote 3690 frames so, every one with an image of every even-sized stream, each matching, into IOSurfaces of their own memory. The Metal platform lends a device writable surfaces over the guest's pages (`CLIENT_MEMORY_LENT`, true, in `crates/reims-vgpu-metal/src/os/device_lend.rs`) with no probe run first: no transfer into an IOSurface over client address ranges has run on Apple's hardware, and none can be a guest's until a VM with the video devices runs on a macOS host. A failure then is fixed forward, or `CLIENT_MEMORY_LENT` set false. The refusals of protected surfaces and of those read-only for their clients stand, since a transfer into a read-only one panicked the M3 Max's kernel. Zero copy is on at every size on VideoToolbox.
  - **Odd sizes on VideoToolbox.** A transfer of 641x361 wrote the last luma column and row as the ones before them, where the CPU copies the frame's own samples (1001 luma samples and 1002 chroma bytes unlike, **measured** on the M3 Max, 2026-10-25), so frames of an odd side are declined, as on radeonsi, and stay on the CPU's copy as a residual. What would fix it: the even part by the transfer and the last column and row by the CPU, which would still copy.
  - **A frame cut to its buffer, or a buffer of another format, on VideoToolbox.** A transfer would scale or convert, so only a frame of the buffer's whole size and format is written; the CPU copies what fits, as before. None of the stand-in guest's streams met it.
  - **Whether VideoToolbox's zero copy should stay on below 4K.** On the M3 Max the transfer takes longer than the CPU's copy at every size: 1.62 ms against 0.17 ms of a 1080p 8-bit frame, 3.13 ms against 1.70 ms of a 4K 10-bit one. It saves the writing thread's CPU only at 4K 10-bit, 0.28 ms against 1.42 ms, and at 1080p costs about as much CPU ([videotoolbox.md](videotoolbox.md), "Zero copy"). It stays on everywhere, as zero copy is wherever a backend allows it, until a guest on a macOS host measures it; if that run shows the same, it goes on only where the copy costs the CPU more than the transfer, 4K 10-bit on the M3 Max.
  - **The decoder writing into the guest's buffer itself**, which Apple's host does, is not built: a decode surface is a reference past the guest's release of its buffer, so it would lengthen the holds the guest sees ([core.md](core.md), "Zero copy").
  - **Not decodes:** the JPEG engine's output, and the encoders' reads of guest surfaces, are copied as before; zero copy covers video decode, and the scaler's kernel reaches its surfaces in place itself ([scaler.md](scaler.md), "The kernel"). The same seam would serve them (`GuestSurface::device_memory`, `crates/reims-vgpu-video-core/src/surfaces.rs`).
  - **Read-only and protected surfaces** are never lent to a device, on purpose: a device must never write pages the guest made read-only (on the M3 Max a write into one panicked the kernel), and a protected surface's memory is not the CPU's to lend.
